# Starting before I felt ready

When I finished college, I knew there was still a lot I needed to learn.

I thought graduating would make me feel ready to work. It did not. I had finished school, but entering the real world was different. I was excited to start, but I was also not sure if what I learned in college was enough.

Still, I knew I had to start somewhere.

## Starting my internship

I started as an IT intern at Seaversity.

During my internship, I began working with real systems and real problems. It was different from the projects we did in school. In school, the instructions were usually clear. At work, sometimes you first need to understand what is wrong before you can even think about fixing it.

Some tasks looked simple until I started doing them. There were also times when I did not know where to begin. I often checked things more than once because I was afraid of making a mistake or breaking something.

I asked questions, searched for answers, and took notes. I slowly learned how to read errors, check logs, and understand the current setup before changing anything.

I also learned that rushing does not prove that you are good at your job. Sometimes, being careful is more important than being fast.

## Doubting myself

There were many times when I doubted myself.

When I received a task that I had never done before, my first thought was usually, "Can I really do this?"

Sometimes I wondered if I was good enough to work in IT. I thought other people already knew what they were doing while I was still trying to understand basic things.

But I later realized that not knowing something does not mean I cannot do the work. It only means I still need to learn it.

I did not always solve problems quickly. Some problems took hours. Some answers were simple, but it took me a long time to find them. Every time I solved something, I became a little more confident.

I still had doubts, but I was slowly learning how to handle them.

## Getting hired

After my internship, I was given the chance to continue working with Seaversity through a project-based role. Later, I was hired as a full-time server administrator.

Written like that, the path sounds simple. I finished college, became an intern, and then got hired. But it did not feel simple while I was going through it.

Becoming a server administrator gave me more responsibilities. I started working more with Linux servers, web services, LMS platforms, domains, reverse proxies, access, and troubleshooting.

I also had opportunities to work on applications and automation, even though my main title was server administrator. This allowed me to learn things outside my original role.

There were still many things I did not know. The difference was that I was becoming more comfortable with learning while doing the work.

## What work taught me

College gave me the basic knowledge I needed to start. Work taught me how to use that knowledge when the answer was not obvious.

I learned not to change something just because I think it might fix the problem. I need to check the current situation first, understand what happened, and think about what could go wrong.

I learned that making mistakes is part of learning. What matters is being honest about the mistake, fixing it, and understanding how to avoid it next time.

I also learned that asking for help is not a bad thing. It is better to ask than to pretend that I understand something when I do not.

Most of the things I know now came from doing the work, facing problems, and trying again when my first solution did not work.

## Why I am writing this

I am not writing this blog because I think I am already an expert.

I want to keep a record of my experience. I want to write about the things I learn, the problems I face, the mistakes I make, and how I slowly figure things out.

Maybe someday I can read these articles again and see how much I have improved.

I also want other students or beginners to know that it is normal to feel unsure when starting. Finishing college does not mean you already know everything. Getting hired does not mean the learning stops.

Sometimes, getting hired is where the real learning begins.

## Still learning

Today, I still have a lot to learn.

I still encounter problems that I do not understand. I still make mistakes, and I still have moments when I doubt myself.

But I do not see those things as proof that I cannot do the work anymore. They are just part of learning.

I started before I felt ready. Looking back, I think that was the only way I could really begin.

If I waited until I knew everything, I would probably still be waiting.
